Direction: Inside WP Admin

Product-led · home page of postquality.com · an option to react to, not a decision

Rationale

The site speaks the plugin's own visual language: WP admin blue, ink and canvas, the system font stack, WP buttons in hero size and WP-style notices. A WordPress user feels at home before installing anything. A large Dashboard capture carries the hero, so the visitor sees their future screen (score, Fix next, trend) before reading a feature list. The review detail further down shows, with numbered notes, exactly what they would click. Medium density, nothing new to learn.

Honest nudges used

  • Prerequisites up front: a WP-style notice sits above the hero buttons, and the full "What you need" block follows the capture, ahead of any install action (path A2).
  • The product as proof, labelled "Demo content": the capture stands in for social proof that does not exist yet.
  • "Your blog" in the capture's admin bar: the visitor pictures their own site. The capture claims no user.
  • You hold the keys: your content goes only to your own n8n and Anthropic, and the plugin never stores the key. A no-cookies, no-analytics line sits beside the email button.
  • "The free plugin is the trial" in the Free vs Pro teaser and in the after-launch hero note.
  • 14-day money-back guarantee and nothing deleted if Pro lapses, side by side under the plans.

Phone: why a focused detail

Scaling the full Dashboard to the 358 px of a 390 px screen would shrink its 13 px admin text to about 4 px. So the phone swaps the capture for a focused detail: the Fix next card, cropped to three rows at real size. Fix next is what the h1 promises, and the real labels stay readable.

What you need before you install

Post Quality runs on your own setup. Check these three things first, and the setup guide will have everything it asks for.

Your own n8n

Self-hosted or n8n Cloud. Your site and n8n must reach each other, so a local or password-protected site can't use n8n Cloud.

An Anthropic API key

The setup guide checks it once and writes it into an n8n credential. The plugin never stores it.

You pay Anthropic per review

On your own key; the prices here cover the plugin only. A quota and a token limit cap what you spend. A worked cost example is coming.
